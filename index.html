Fueling a triathlon shouldn't need a spreadsheet.

Too many products

Gels, salts, powders, capsules. Most triathletes build a plan from forums, friends and trial and error, then pay for the mistakes.

Timing nobody explains

Three sports means three sets of needs, and every race distance changes them again. Each kit comes with a one-page plan: what to take, when, and how much.

Safety you can't check

A contaminated supplement can end a season. Informed Sport says as many as one in ten supplements can contain substances banned in sport. Our plan is to test every batch before it ships.

Five kits. One for each situation.

Each kit answers one question: what do I take for this? The bars show how long the race is, split into swim, bike and run.

    Kit contents are a concept. Amounts are set with a sports dietitian before production. Prices are targets from the business plan.

    At launchMonths 6 to 12

    Marketing is about 18% of year 1 revenue. A customer who buys two kits returns about €45 after production and shipping, roughly three times what it costs to win them. Paid social will probably cost more per customer than clubs and word of mouth, so the team tracks each channel.

    Seven risks, and what we would do about each.

    The biggest financial risk is selling less than planned: 25% fewer kits turns the year 1 profit into a loss of about €8,940.

    Three owners and four outside experts.

    Each person owns one area and supports the others. Experts cover the parts that need professional knowledge.

    • A sports dietitian approves kit contents and amounts.
    • A food-law expert reviews labels, claims and the notification to the BVL (the German food safety authority).
    • A tax advisor handles company setup, VAT and bookkeeping.
    • The contract manufacturer produces and packs each batch.
